Unpublished workshop material, not a queue or evidence of posting. Ten candidates, followed by an editorial selection. These are original drafts generated in this session, with originality checks still limited by search coverage.

## Candidates

1. **Spreadsheet acquaintance:** I'm familiar with Excel in the sense that if I saw it in a supermarket I'd take a different aisle.
   - The concrete scene makes a tired software complaint more specific. Check the familiarity-as-acquaintance premise, not just exact words. May still be too familiar.
2. **Rehearsal logic, first version:** If we're pretending there's a fire, I don't see why we can't pretend I left.
   - The rat earnestly extends the premise until the exercise becomes useless. Standalone fictional comedy, not safety advice. Stronger than adding a whole fictional story about being reprimanded.
3. **Museum assessment:** They've ruled out liking it.
   - A possible Quote Post caption for the photographed label `Nasty objects of some sort`: https://x.com/TyrantLzrdQueen/status/1603142111976644609. The source is from December 2022, not current news. The author identifies the material in follow-up posts as unidentified ice-age mammal remains; the caption concerns the label's judgment, not a false archaeological claim.
4. **Working week:** I support the four-day workweek. The fifth day is when my mistakes usually become policy.
   - A self-own rather than generic management advice. Somewhat neat and formulaic; compare against the more distinctive candidates.
5. **Wall complaint:** My landlord called the wall "load-bearing." I'd noticed it had a job. I was asking about the hole.
   - The second sentence introduces an amusing category mistake, but the final complaint drags the joke back into a conventional landlord grievance. Too much setup.
6. **Concrete proposal:** The board asked for a more concrete proposal. I've added an estimated weight.
   - Understandable but a basic literal-idiom pun. Not enough surprise to justify publication.
7. **Aquarium notice:** The aquarium has a sign explaining that the shark is meant to lie motionless on the floor. I would like one of those for my office.
   - A hypothetical comic scene, not a sourced claim about a particular aquarium. The workplace-excuse finish is familiar and weaker than the opening image.
8. **Secret society:** Before I join your secret society I'd like to know why there's a newsletter.
   - Probably a well-worn secrecy/publicity contradiction. Reject rather than spend research time trying to rescue a familiar premise.
9. **Quiet room:** Our office has a quiet room. So far only the loudest people have thought to announce they're using it.
   - Observationally clear, but more complaint than surprise. Reject.
10. **Lab contamination:** The lab says the sample was contaminated by a human. Finally, a professional assessment.
    - Fits the rat without saying rat; however, the last sentence is a vague approval reaction and the joke risks becoming routine anti-human mascot material. Reject.

## Editing principle from this pass

The character should make a specific, slightly wrong inference, not merely express an opinion in a formal tone. Prefer concrete scenes to abstract claims about committees. Keep an external item's context attached when it supplies the setup.

## Selection

- Selected candidate 2 as RAT-007. Final: `If we're pretending there's a fire, I don't see why we can't pretend I'm outside.` The location is a cleaner final image than the completed action `I left`. Rejected a shorter variant, `Fire drills are very strict about which part you're allowed to imagine`, because it shifts from a character trying something to a detached observation.
- Selected candidate 3 as RAT-008, preserving the source as a Quote Post. Considered `Unidentified, but not unjudged`, but rejected the polished parallel phrasing in favor of the plainer `They've ruled out liking it.`
- Rejected candidate 1 after searching both its distinctive supermarket wording on the web and `familiar with Excel` on X. No matching aisle joke was visible, but the broad software-acquaintance turn feels less distinctive than the two selections. No claim that it was plagiarized.
- Rejected candidate 4 as a neat but less surprising workplace self-own. Candidates 5–10 rejected for the reasons above.
- Web and X checks of candidates 2 and 3 found no obvious matching joke in the inspected results, not proof of originality. Relevant queries and limitations are recorded beside the selected drafts.

Only selected candidates belong in `QUEUE.md`; do not reintroduce rejected workshop lines automatically.
