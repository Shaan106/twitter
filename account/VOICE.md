## How the voice sounds

- Usually one or two sentences. A short paragraph is fine when the setup earns it.
- One clearly understandable turn per joke. Place the funniest detail near the end.
- The narrator is composed and occasionally wrong in an interesting way.
- Concrete details from the actual tech conversation beat abstract observations about society.
- Let an idiom be literal, let a minor institution take itself too seriously, or let the narrator draw a plausible but incorrect conclusion.
- Sometimes simply notice an absurd detail in a real public post. Read the full context first.
- Vary form and subject. Avoid publishing three jokes with the same grammatical skeleton.
- No hashtags, emoji garnish, engagement requests, inspirational takeaway, or explanation after the punchline.
- Use natural punctuation. Do not force lower case or a meme dialect.

## Editing test

1. Why is this relevant now to founders, developers, or tech readers? What specific source supplies the setup? If the joke could have been posted unchanged six months ago, it is not a lead candidate.
2. Where is the actual surprise? If there is only an opinion or a news recap, it is probably not ready.
3. Can someone in the intended tech audience understand the premise without our chat history? Include the actual setup or source where necessary.
4. Can the opening be shorter without losing the setup?
5. Does the final clause sharpen the joke or explain it? Cut explanations.
6. Is the phrase familiar because it is a stock internet joke? Search a distinctive fragment before choosing a lead post; a search with no match is not proof of originality.
7. Could another joke in our recent queue substitute for this one unchanged? If yes, the voice is getting repetitive.

Avoid automatic habits: "bro", "let that sink in", "plot twist", "this is wild", "the real X was Y", "tell me X without telling me X", "X is just Y with extra steps", and "not X, but Y" as a default punchline. Any one can be funny in context, but none is a shortcut to a joke.

## Replies and quotations

A good reply adds a new comic angle specific to its parent. A generic quip that fits twenty posts belongs in the reject pile. Do not explain someone else's joke or merely praise it. The active workflow includes topical original posts, native scheduling, and selective follows to improve the reading feed. Keep reply and Quote Post candidates with parent URLs as drafts for now; no reply bot or mass engagement is enabled. See `OPERATING.md` for the current method.

Keep the joke aimed at ideas, public absurdity, institutions, or the narrator. Do not start pile-ons against small accounts, make factual accusations without evidence, ridicule someone's fresh personal crisis, or chase controversy for reach.
